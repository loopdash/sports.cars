Porsche 911 Buyer's Guide: Which Model Is Right for You?

By James Whitfield · May 6, 2026 · 8 min read

The Porsche 911 is one of the most enduring nameplates in automotive history — but "911" spans dozens of variants across eight generations, and the right one depends entirely on how you plan to drive it. This guide breaks the modern lineup into the choices that actually matter.

Know your generations

Since 2019, the 992-generation 911 has defined the current range, succeeding the widely loved 991. Both are excellent, but the 992 brought wider bodywork, a refreshed interior, and updated turbocharged flat-six engines across the board.[1]

Carrera, S, or GTS?

The base Carrera is far from basic — its 3.0L twin-turbo flat-six delivers effortless daily performance. Step up to the Carrera S for more power and larger brakes, or the GTS for the enthusiast's balance of sharper responses without sacrificing usability.

  • Carrera — 379 hp, the most comfortable everyday choice.
  • Carrera S — 443 hp, the popular all-rounder.
  • GTS — 473 hp, the driver's pick.
If you can only test-drive one, make it the GTS. It reframes what you think a 911 should feel like.

Going for the GT cars

The GT3 and GT3 RS sit at the top for track-focused buyers, trading turbochargers for a high-revving naturally aspirated engine and motorsport-derived aerodynamics.[2] They command significant premiums on the used market and rarely depreciate.

The verdict

For most buyers, a 992 Carrera S is the 911 to own — quick, comfortable, and endlessly usable. Choose the GTS if you prioritize engagement, and the GT3 only if track days are a genuine part of your plans.
